Germany's defence industry is recruiting. In the first half of 2026 the sector posted [about 4,700 job ads](https://www.index.de/pressemitteilungen/ruestungsindustrie-schreibt-22-mehr-jobs-aus/), 22% more than a year earlier.

Engineers alone do not build vehicles, radar and munitions. The work needs mechatronics technicians, machinists and electronics technicians, trades that in Germany are learned through the dual apprenticeship system. In exactly these trades, a growing share of apprenticeship contracts is cancelled early, most of them in the first year.

This article sets out the figures trade by trade, and shows where employers can make the biggest difference.

![Comparison: 22 percent more job ads in Germany's defence industry in the first half of 2026, 9.1 percent fewer new apprenticeship contracts in metal and electrical trades in 2025](/images/blog/ruestung-bedarf-nachwuchs.svg)

## Germany's apprenticeship system in brief

If you work outside Germany, three terms help to read the figures:

- **Ausbildung** is the German apprenticeship. In the trades covered here it normally takes three and a half years. Apprentices are employed and paid by a company, trained on site and spend part of their time at a vocational school.
- **The chambers of industry and commerce (IHK)** organise and grade the final exams. Passing makes the apprentice a qualified skilled worker.
- **BIBB**, the Federal Institute for Vocational Education and Training, publishes the national statistics used below.

For a defence company with plants in Germany, this system is the main source of skilled production staff. It is also where the pipeline is under pressure.

**How to read the table:**

- **The talent pool is small.** In 2025, about 36,500 new apprenticeship contracts were signed across all eight trades combined. Defence companies, machine builders and automotive suppliers all compete for the same young people.
- **More are lost than before.** In seven of the eight trades, the share of contracts cancelled early has risen since 2022. For CNC machinists, more than one contract in four now ends early. The only exception is aircraft mechanics: after 18.4% in 2023, the rate fell to 12.3% in 2024.
- **The exam is rarely the obstacle.** In all eight trades, more than 92% of final exams are passed. The problem lies at the start of the apprenticeship, not at the end.

## More vacancies, fewer apprentices

The demand is clear. According to Index Research, almost 1,600 of the sector's 4,700 job ads were for technical specialists. The locations with the most ads were Unterlüß, Kassel and Schrobenhausen, home to major Rheinmetall, KNDS and MBDA sites. The German Economic Institute's [skills shortage centre (KOFA)](https://www.iwkoeln.de/studien/jurek-tiedemann-gero-kunath-fachkraeftereport-maerz-2026-weniger-engpaesse-in-sozialberufen-mehr-bei-infrastruktur-und-verteidigung.html) also sees growing shortages in metal and electrical jobs, for example in welding, metalworking and machine building. It links the rising demand partly to Germany's special funds for defence and infrastructure.

The apprentice intake is moving the other way. According to the [IG Metall apprenticeship report](https://www.igmetall.de/jugend/auszubildende/ausbildungsbilanz-industrie-spart-am-nachwuchs), about 25,800 apprenticeship contracts were signed in the classic metal and electrical trades in 2025. That is 9.1% fewer than the year before and almost a fifth fewer than in 2019.

Notably, there are applicants. On 30 September 2025, [39,900 young people were still looking for an apprenticeship](https://www.bibb.de/dienst/publikationen/de/21037), the highest number since records began in 2007. Employers and applicants often fail to find each other. And even when they do, the signed contract is only the start.

## Why career changers will not close the gap

Many hope that workers from the car industry, where jobs are being cut, will fill the gap. A study by the Institute for Employment Research (IAB) tempers that hope. Last year, only [about 1,100 employees from other industrial sectors moved into the defence industry](https://www.handelsblatt.com/politik/deutschland/industrie-arbeitsmarkt-iab-studie-ruestung-kann-jobverluste-nicht-ausgleichen/100256133.html) nationwide. That is three times as many as before 2020. Set against the roughly 15,000 industrial jobs currently lost each month, it is still small.

For most employers, the conclusion is simple: they will have to train tomorrow's skilled workers themselves. And they will have to keep them.

## The exam is not the problem

If you only look at the final exam, apprenticeships look healthy. 92 to 96% of exams are passed.

That figure has a blind spot. It only covers exams that are actually taken. An apprentice who leaves in the first year never appears in it.

That group is growing. Across all trades, BIBB reports that 29.7% of apprenticeship contracts were cancelled early in 2024. All eight trades in the table are below that, which is the good news. The bad news is that the trend points upwards. For a company with 20 apprentices per intake, 16% means about three lost contracts, year after year.

**Cancelled early does not mean dropped out.** BIBB itself stresses that its cancellation rate "is not a dropout rate". Many apprentices simply change employer or trade and qualify elsewhere. For the company that hired, onboarded and paid them, the investment is gone all the same.

## The first year decides

When do contracts end? The [BIBB Datenreport 2026](https://www.bibb.de/dienst/publikationen/de/21037) gives a clear answer. This is how all contracts cancelled early in 2024 were distributed:

![Stacked bars: of all apprenticeship contracts cancelled early in 2024, 36.5 percent in industry and commerce ended during probation and 33.9 percent later in the first year; across all trades 34.7 and 32.9 percent](/images/blog/ruestung-zeitpunkt-vertragsloesung.svg)

In industry and commerce, which includes the metal and electrical trades, about 70% of all cancelled contracts end within the first twelve months.

Picture a mechatronics apprentice in their third month. A new workshop, new colleagues, vocational school, and electrical engineering for the first time. They can no longer follow Ohm's law. They have already asked three times and do not dare ask a fourth. Nobody notices from the outside. This is how the path out of a contract can begin.

Of course, not every contract ends because of the subject matter. No learning aid fixes a conflict at work or the wrong choice of career. But much of what an employer can influence happens in this first year.

## How to compare your company with the figures

The table becomes useful once you put your own numbers next to it. A simple calculation per trade is enough:

1. **Choose your intakes.** Take all apprentices in one trade who started at least two years ago, for example all mechatronics apprentices from the 2021 to 2023 intakes. About 90% of all cancellations happen in the first two years, so these intakes are almost complete.
2. **Count the cancelled contracts.** Whether the apprentice resigned, you terminated, or both sides agreed to end it.
3. **Divide.** Cancelled contracts divided by contracts started gives your rate.

BIBB uses a different method, so your result is an approximation. It is good enough for an honest assessment. If you are well above 16% for industrial mechanics, a closer look is worthwhile. If you are below, you are clearly doing a lot right. With only a few apprentices a year, the figures fluctuate strongly, so combine several intakes.

Finally, look at when the contracts ended. If most cases fall within the first twelve months, you know where to start.

### What a cancelled contract costs

For the conversation with management or finance, two figures from the [BIBB cost-benefit survey](https://www.bibb.de/de/pressemitteilung_210430.php) for the 2022/23 training year help:

- **An apprentice costs a German employer about €26,200 a year on average.** Apprentices recover about 70% of that through productive work, leaving a net cost of about €8,100.
- **Recruiting a skilled worker externally costs about €13,700 on average.** Compared with earlier BIBB surveys, these recruiting costs have risen by 65%, the net cost of training by only 28%.

![Bar chart: an apprentice costs about 26,200 euros gross and 8,100 euros net per year, recruiting a skilled worker externally about 13,700 euros](/images/blog/ruestung-kosten-ausbildung-vs-extern.svg)

Both are averages across all trades. The direction is still clear: an apprentice who leaves in the first year costs you twice. Once for training that produces no skilled worker, and once for the position you later fill, at higher cost, from outside. ## 5 things to do differently with your next intake

**1. Use the probation period for conversations.** Schedule fixed check-ins in weeks 2, 6 and 12. Ask specific questions instead of "Is everything fine?". Our [apprentice onboarding guide](/blog/apprentice-onboarding) has a checklist for the first eight weeks.

**2. Do not leave theory to the vocational school alone.** In the electrical trades in particular, gaps open early and then widen. Short, regular refreshers at work close them, ideally aligned with what the school is teaching.

**3. Watch for early signals.** More absences, thinner training logs, an apprentice who suddenly stops asking questions. A [digital training logbook](/blog/digital-training-logbook) shows such patterns earlier than a paper folder that reaches your desk at the end of the month.

**4. Support your trainers instead of asking more of them.** Trainers who look after growing intakes with the same headcount have less time for individual conversations. We have collected seven approaches in [Reduce trainer workload](/blog/reduce-trainer-workload).

**5. Involve the works council early.** In Germany, data on apprentices' learning progress is a matter for co-determination with the works council. Set up digital tools as support rather than monitoring from the start, and agree the rules together.
