# Copy analysis: the AI guides and their download page

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Surfaces analysed:** `/ai-guides` landing page, both guide covers, the form, the
closing offer page inside each guide.
**Copy score: 78/100**

## How this differs from a standard copy audit

The skill this ran from is built for a conversion page, and most of its playbook
applies here. Three parts of it do not, and adopting them would damage the asset.

**Urgency and scarcity are out.** "Claim my spot, 12 left" works on a webinar. On a
guide whose entire position is that it does not oversell, to an audience where a third
of non-users say they do not trust AI output, manufactured urgency reads as the thing
they are already suspicious of. There is no deadline here and inventing one would be
the first dishonest sentence on a page that has none.

**First-person CTA buttons are out.** "Get my free guide" tests well in general. This
page already says "Show me the guide", which is first person without the possessive
tic, and the audience is senior enough that "my free" reads as consumer funnel.

**Testimonials and logo walls are out.** The guides' proof is a named author with 17+
years and every figure traced to its publisher. Adding a logo wall would dilute a
stronger signal with a weaker one.

Everything else in the playbook is applied below.

## Headline recommendations

**Current, landing page:** "97% of product managers say AI made them faster. Only 64%
say it made their product better."

This is already good and should stay. It is ultra-specific, it is a real finding, and
the gap does the emotional work without an adjective. Alternatives for testing only:

| # | Alternative | Framework |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Keep the current headline* | Data-led contrast |
| 2 | AI made 97% of product managers faster. It made 64% of their products better. | Same fact, active voice |
| 3 | Everyone got faster. Not everyone shipped anything better. | PAS, compressed |
| 4 | The 33 points between working quicker and shipping better | 4U, ultra-specific |
| 5 | Faster is not the same as better. Here is the difference, in five places. | Before-After-Bridge |
| 6 | Your Copilot probably cannot read a word of your work | Curiosity, pulled from Part 0 |
| 7 | Two guides. Every number traced to the body that published it. | Mechanism-led |
| 8 | What AI actually changes in a product week, and what it does not | Balanced promise |
| 9 | 677 product professionals, 40 countries, one uncomfortable gap | Proof-led |
| 10 | The guide that tells you where AI is not worth your time | Contrarian |

Ranked for this audience: 1, 2, 5, 7. Numbers 3 and 10 are punchier and would cost
credibility with a sceptical reader.

## Before and after

### 1. The mechanism, landing page third paragraph

**Before:** "Every figure is traced to the body that published it. Nothing is estimated,
and nothing before the last page is an advertisement."

**After:** "Every number in these guides was traced back to the organisation that
published it: Product Focus, the Scrum Guide, Microsoft's own documentation. Nothing is
estimated. Nothing before the last page is an advertisement."

**Why:** naming three sources converts an abstract claim into checkable proof at no cost
in length. The current version asks the reader to take the discipline on trust, which is
the one thing this page should never ask.

### 2. Author credibility, currently absent from the landing page

**Before:** *(nothing on the page names the author)*

**After:** "Written by Alesia Kunz, CEO of LearnSlice, after 17+ years as a product
manager and product owner. The field notes throughout are hers."

**Why:** the guides carry a named author with a photograph and the landing page does
not mention her. That is the single largest missed proof element on the page.

### 3. The qualifying question microcopy

**Before:** "This is the only thing we ask for in return."

**After:** "This is the only thing we ask for in return. It decides nothing about your
download."

**Why:** the sentence currently reads as a soft obligation, and a reader who wants the
guide but not a conversation may hesitate over a required field. Saying that the answer
does not gate the file removes the hesitation and makes the honest answer more likely,
which is the entire point of collecting it.

### 4. The result state

**Before:** "Here it is."

**After:** "Here it is. 24 pages, and the twenty-minute path is on page three."

**Why:** the moment after conversion is where a reader decides whether to open the file
now or never. Naming the short path converts a download into a read.

### 5. Meta description

**Before:** "Two practical guides on using AI in product work, benchmarked against
published research. What the tools can do, what they cannot, and the prompts worth
keeping."

**After:** "97% of product managers say AI made them faster. Only 64% say it made their
product better. Two practical guides on closing that gap, with every figure traced to
its source."

**Why:** the current description leads with category. The revision leads with the
finding, which is the thing that earns a click, and keeps the mechanism.

## CTA analysis

| CTA | Current | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | "Show me the guide" | Keep. Specific, first person without the possessive, no risk language needed because there is no risk |
| Result | "Download the PDF" | Keep, but see before-and-after 4 for the line above it |
| In-guide close | "Reply to the email this arrived with" and the form pointer | Correct restraint. One ask, on the last page |

**Placement:** there is one CTA and it sits below four benefit lines. For a page this
short that is right. Do not add a sticky bar.

**Missing:** no CTA above the fold on a phone, because the headline plus intro plus four
benefits push the form down. Worth testing a single line under the headline that jumps
to the form.

## Implementation priority

| Rank | Change | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add the author to the landing page | **Applied** |
| 2 | Name the three sources in the mechanism line | **Applied** |
| 3 | Rewrite the meta description to lead with the finding | **Applied** |
| 4 | Add the reassurance to the qualifying question | **Applied** |
| 5 | Name the page count and the short path in the result state | **Applied** |
| 6 | Anchor link to the form on phones | **Closed, not needed** |

Item 6 was conditional on the form sitting far down a phone screen. It does not. The
rendered DOM carries only four blocks between the headline and the form, and contains
no fixed width above 380px, so the form is one short scroll away and there is no
horizontal overflow risk. An anchor would add a control for a problem that is not
there.

Nothing here was a rewrite. The copy was in good shape; the fixes were proof placement.

## One judgement call left open

Emotion scored lowest, at 6 of 10, because the landing page flattens after the
headline. The author paragraph now carries some of that, but the four numbered benefit
lines are still a list of features written plainly. They could be rewritten as
tensions, the way the guide's own chapter titles are, for example "which Copilot tier
can actually see your work" becoming "why most people have the wrong Copilot and never
find out".

This is deliberately not in the priority list. The flat register is doing real work for
an audience that distrusts AI claims, and turning four benefit lines into four hooks
risks the page sounding like the marketing the guides position themselves against.
Worth testing, not worth assuming.
