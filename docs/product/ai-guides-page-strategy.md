# /ai-guides: marketing and sales strategy

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Replaces:** the three-offer approach in `ai-guide-conversion-design.md`, which
assumed a workshop push. No workshop is promoted anywhere.

---

## The position

**LearnSlice is a learning company that builds with AI.** Teaching people to use
these tools well is the work, not a wrapper around it. That is the whole reason
the guides can be free and complete without the page feeling like a trade.

It follows that the page sells indirectly or not at all. A reader learns
something here, applies it to their own week, and finds out for themselves
whether we are any good. Nothing else we could write would demonstrate that.

**The through-line is learn, then apply.** The guides are built for it: every
chapter ends with a first step, and Part 4 is a thirty minute weekly routine.
That is also the differentiator against the prompt-list competitors, who supply
material to read and nothing to practise.

Three things are available to a reader, and they differ by who moves first:

| | What it is | Who moves first |
|---|---|---|
| The guides | Free, complete, sourced. No gate on the knowledge itself | Nobody. It is given |
| Advice or support | A question answered by a named person | The reader |
| The AI academy | In build. Teams apply | The reader applies, we select |
| Their own academy | Built on their content and brand. The existing white-label motion | The reader |
| AI software engineering | Custom work on a company's own data | The reader |

Note what is missing: there is no offer the reader can be pushed into. Every
one of the three requires them to move. That is the whole anti-desperation
mechanism, and it is structural rather than a matter of tone.

---

## Why "apply" rather than "buy"

The academy is in build and runs with a small number of teams. Saying so plainly
does three things at once:

1. It is true, which matters more than it should in this category.
2. It inverts the usual dynamic. A seller persuades; a selector qualifies. The
   reader arrives at a decision rather than being moved to one.
3. It costs nothing to say and cannot be copied by a competitor who is
   already selling seats at volume.

The word is **apply**, never *sign up*, *book*, *claim* or *reserve*.

---

## The engine is the weekly, not the download

The download is a single transaction. The weekly email is a relationship, and
it is the only thing on this page that compounds.

A reader who takes the guide and leaves is worth one PDF. A reader who takes
the weekly is in contact for months, during which we never once have to chase
them, because the email is useful on its own terms. By the time they have an
AI problem worth paying for, they already know who to ask.

**This means the weekly, not the guide, is the conversion that matters.** The
guide's job is to earn the right to ask for it.

---

## Rules that keep it from reading as desperate

Desperation is not a tone. It is a set of behaviours, and each has an
observable opposite.

| Desperate | What we do instead |
|---|---|
| Several CTAs competing on one screen | One primary action. The weekly is a checkbox inside it, not a second button |
| "Book a free strategy call" | No call offered anywhere. An email address and a name |
| Countdown timers, "limited spots" | The academy is genuinely limited, so say the fact and add no pressure to it |
| Testimonial wall | None. This audience discounts them, and we have none worth showing |
| Re-asking on the success screen | The success screen asks for nothing. It hands over the file and says forwarding is fine |
| Claiming a finished product | The academy is in build. Say so |
| Chasing the download by email | The file is not emailed. Nothing is sent that was not asked for |

The last one is worth keeping even though it costs a contact point. "We do not
email you the file" is the single most credible sentence on the page, because
every competitor does the opposite.

## What the page says about the three offers

Once, near the end, after the reader has had everything for free. Stated as
fact, not as a pitch, and in this order, because it runs from smallest ask to
largest:

1. **A question.** Named person, real address. No form, no call booking.
2. **The academy.** In build, teams apply, content matched to the sector.
3. **Their own academy.** Built on their content and brand. This is the existing
   white-label motion and it is a larger engagement than joining ours.
4. **Engineering.** Custom AI on a company's own data, EU or on-premise.

No section promotes a workshop. If a reader wants one, the question route
already exists and they will ask.

## What we measure

The probe question on the form still runs, with options matched to the three
offers above. Read it as demand research rather than as a lead queue.

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Weekly opt-in rate | Whether the guide earned the relationship. **The primary number** |
| Split across the three offers | Which business line the audience actually wants |
| Share picking "just the guide" | Honest baseline. A high share is information, not failure |
| Replies to the weekly | The strongest buying signal available, and unsolicited |

If the weekly opt-in rate is low, the guide is not landing and no amount of
offer copy will fix it. Check that before touching anything else.

---

## What this means for search

The strategy removes the commercial keyword clusters from scope. We are not
bidding for "AI workshop", we are not competing for "AI academy" against
Product School, and the academy is application-gated anyway so ranking for a
buying query would attract the wrong reader.

**Search only has to do one job here: bring product managers and product
owners who want an answer.** That makes the target purely informational, and it
makes the weakly-defended product owner cluster the whole opportunity. Keyword
work should be re-run against that narrower brief.
