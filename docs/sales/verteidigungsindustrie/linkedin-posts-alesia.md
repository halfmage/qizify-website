## Week 1 (Mon 5 Oct 2026): the pipeline squeeze

**Post text**

Germany's defence companies are hiring 22% more people than a year ago.

The apprentice pipeline they depend on is shrinking from both ends.

Fewer start. Companies signed 9.1% fewer apprenticeship contracts in the classic metal and electrical trades in 2025.

More leave. The share of training contracts cancelled early rose every year from 2022 to 2024 (nationwide, all industries):

Industrial mechanics: 12.3% → 16.2%
Electronics technicians: 14.3% → 18.6%
CNC machinists: 21.8% → 26.0%
Metal fabricators: 26.8% → 27.8%

And the hope that laid-off car workers will fill the gap? The IAB counted only about 1,100 people who moved from other industries into defence last year.

The strange part: the exams are fine. 92 to 96% of final exams in these trades are passed. A pass rate simply does not count the people who already left.

Not every cancelled contract is a dropout. Many start another apprenticeship. For the company that trained them, it is still months and money gone.

Software will not fix a bad workshop culture or the wrong career choice. I am not going to pretend it does.

What it can fix: the apprentice who gets lost in electrical theory in month three and is too embarrassed to ask a fourth time. That is who we build LearnSlice for. And in defence, it runs in Germany or on the company's own servers.

My bet: the companies that win this talent race will not be the ones that hire the most apprentices. They will be the ones that keep them.

👉 Trainers in metal and electrical trades: what is the real reason apprentices leave in your company?

## Week 2 (Mon 12 Oct 2026): they do not leave for a better offer

**Post text**

Apprentices who think about quitting rarely have a better offer.

Only 13% of them name one. The most common reason: 36% say the training weighs on them mentally.

That is from the Bertelsmann Stiftung's new survey of 1,325 apprentices (spring 2026). Almost one in four had seriously thought about quitting.

The other reasons they gave:

😶 30%: "the training is completely different from what I expected"
⏱️ 28%: no time left for anything else
📚 24%: unhappy with how the content is taught

And timing matters. BIBB data shows two thirds of all early cancellations happen in the first 12 months. More than a third during probation.

So picture year one in a metal or electrical trade. New people, new shifts, a loud workshop. Technical drawings, materials, electrical basics, maths many last saw at school. And a trainer who is onboarding a whole new group at the same time.

If the first sign a trainer gets is a resignation letter, it is too late.

I will be straight about the limits: an AI tutor is not mental-health support, and it must never replace the conversation with a trainer.

What it can take away is one specific pressure: feeling lost in the theory and having nobody to ask at 9 pm. And it can show the trainer which topics are causing trouble, while there is still time to talk.

That is support, not surveillance. Set it up with your works council from day one.

## Week 3 (Mon 19 Oct 2026): faster is not the same as understanding

**Post text**

Apprentices with ChatGPT solved more tasks, faster, and felt less frustrated.

They did not understand the material any better.

That is the result of a BIBB experiment with 27 second-year IT apprentices, published in the Datenreport 2026. Many copied the AI's answer without checking it. Some of it was wrong.

Now picture a mechatronics apprentice.

Year one at vocational school alone: mechanical systems, electrical installation under safety rules, energy flows in electrical and hydraulic systems, data processing. Around 320 hours of theory across four disciplines.

Part 1 of the final exam comes before the end of year two. It counts for 40% of the final grade.

An apprentice who copies answers through that year will pass quizzes and fail at the machine.

This is why we built LearnSlice to ask before it tells:

🧐 It asks what the apprentice already knows, then guides them to the answer
🔗 Every explanation points to its source in the curriculum
🎓 The trainer decides which material the tutor may use
🔐 Hosted in Germany or on-premise, learner data never trains the model

The honest limit: no tutor replaces hands on the workpiece. Mechatronics is learned at the machine. The tutor's job is to make sure the theory is there when the apprentice gets to it.

My prediction: within two years, the question for training managers will not be "should apprentices use AI". They already do. It will be "which AI makes them think, and which one thinks for them".
