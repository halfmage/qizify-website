## 1. Review findings (what was wrong with v1)

1. **The market is small if we only count Azubis at defence primes.** Roughly 1,000 starters a year across the big names, so "defence Azubis" alone cannot carry a pipeline. Defence is the beachhead story, the real market is **industrial metal and electrical (M+E) Ausbildung**: 35,367 Industriemechaniker, 28,080 Mechatroniker, 23,427 Elektroniker Betriebstechnik in training (BIBB, 31.12.2024). The SEO content should serve that whole market, with defence as the proof and urgency angle.
2. **The primes are the wrong first customer.** They have group L&D (Hensoldt's Learning Space already runs LinkedIn Learning), central procurement, IT security reviews, and they will ask for ISO 27001 / ISMS / pen-test. We have none of these today (`docs/grants/agentic-ai-hub-fit.md`). A prime deal in Q4 is not realistic.
3. **Job-title keywords attract applicants, not buyers.** "Industriemechaniker Ausbildung" is dominated by job boards and Arbeitsagentur. Buyer intent reads "Mechatroniker ausbilden", "Ausbildungsabbruch verhindern", "Ausbilder entlasten". Slugs are changed accordingly.
4. **"We reduce dropout" is a claim we cannot prove yet.** Most contract cancellations have causes a tutor cannot touch (conflicts in the company, wrong career choice, working conditions). Promise **early warning + learning support**, never "cut dropout by X %".
5. **Content-only was too passive for a named-account market.** With a few hundred relevant decision makers, account-based selling through warm channels beats waiting for inbound. v2 adds partners, associations and a pilot offer.
6. **English LinkedIn does not reach German Ausbildungsleiter well.** Accepted as a decision; the English posts are aimed at who reads English (see 3.2), and German happens in 1:1 messages and comments.

## 3. How we win

### 3.1 The offer

- **Promise:** "Your Azubis get a tutor built on the Ausbildungsrahmenplan and your own documents; your trainers see early who is falling behind."
- **Sovereignty, made concrete:** German hosting or fully on-prem, open-weight model, no data leaves the company, every answer traceable to a source. In defence this is the entry ticket; everyone says "DSGVO", few can say "runs on your own servers".
- **Start with non-sensitive content.** Pilot on the public IHK curriculum only, company documents come after their security review. This removes the export-control objection from the first deal.
- **Paid pilot with clear success criteria:** one Beruf, one cohort, 8 to 10 weeks. Measure trainer hours saved, AP Teil 1 mock scores, usage, and early-warning cases flagged. Small fixed fee, credited against a yearly licence. (Price to be set.)
- **Career-changer package:** the same engine fed with plant documents and safety instructions, measured by time to independent work. Check public funding for employer-sponsored upskilling (SGB III) before pitching it as a cost lever.

### 3.2 Channels, in order of expected yield

1. **Warm networks:** IHK Ausbilder-Arbeitskreise in defence regions (Celle/Lüneburg, Nordhessen, Oberbayern, Schwaben, Kiel, Bodensee, Saarland), M+E employer associations and their training arms, regional aerospace and defence clusters (e.g. BavAIRia). Offer a 30-minute talk on AI in M+E Ausbildung, not a pitch.
2. **LinkedIn 1:1 (German):** 10 personal connection requests per week to named Ausbildungsleiter and HR at the target accounts, 5 comments on their posts. Follows the UWG-safe rules in `docs/sales/bildungstraeger/linkedin-and-inbound-playbook.md`.
3. **LinkedIn posts (English, Alesia):** for readers who read English: group L&D and HR at international primes, the European defence and edtech network, partners, grant bodies. They are Alesia's credibility layer when a German prospect checks her profile after a 1:1 message.
4. **SEO (German):** long-term inbound for the whole M+E Ausbildung market, and pages sales can send after a call.
5. **Events (plan for 2027 H1):** defence and training trade fairs, AFCEA, regional Ausbildungsmessen as visitor, not exhibitor.

### 3.3 Readiness gates before approaching anyone beyond priority 1

- One-page security fact sheet: hosting location and provider, on-prem option, model used, data flows, AVV, TOMs, who can access what (including delivery team in Poland).
- Decision on ISO 27001 path (the primes will ask; a roadmap with a date beats "no").
- One demo built on a real M+E curriculum (Mechatroniker AP Teil 1).
- Objection answers: "we already have an LMS", "IT security will say no", "Azubis should learn from the trainer, not a chatbot", "what about export control".

## 4. Verified data to reuse (do not replace with provider-blog numbers)

BIBB DAZUBI Datenblätter (Berichtsjahr 2024, Stand 28.10.2025) and BIBB Tabelle 67 (Neuabschlüsse 2025).

**Wording rule:** the Lösungsquote is the share of training contracts cancelled early. BIBB states explicitly it is *not* a dropout rate: many of these Azubis continue in another company or Beruf. Always write "contracts cancelled early" / "vorzeitig gelöste Verträge", never "x % drop out". For the company it is still a lost investment, which is the point we make.

**More rules:** EQ I counts exam attempts passed, so never write "first try". Publish trends as 2022 to 2024 only (2008 sits across a BIBB method change). Say "national, all industries" wherever BIBB figures sit next to defence. Timing of cancellations: BIBB Datenreport 2025, A5.6 (2023: 35.3 % in probation, 33.3 % later in year one).
